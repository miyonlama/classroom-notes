Logic and Proof Methods — Important Questions

Questions from the nine past papers (2078–2082). Frequency and marks are transcribed from Past Questions.pdf. Answers will be added after review.

How to read a card

  • Marks lists every distinct mark the question carried. A question with parts can have more than one value.
  • A repeated question appears once, with all its occurrences in the Frequency line.

Explain direct proof, indirect proof, and proof by contradiction. Use a direct proof to show: “If n is an odd integer, then n² is an odd integer.” Also use an indirect proof to show: “If n is an integer and n² is odd, then n is odd.”

Frequency: 2078 (Q1) · Marks: 10

Direct proof. To prove p → q, assume p and derive q by algebra and known facts. Indirect proof proves p → q by proving the contrapositive ¬q → ¬p. Proof by contradiction assumes ¬p together with the premises and derives a contradiction.

Direct: n odd ⇒ n² odd. Write n = 2k + 1. Then n² = 4k² + 4k + 1 = 2(2k² + 2k) + 1, which is twice an integer plus 1, hence odd.

Indirect: n² odd ⇒ n odd. Prove the contrapositive: if n is even then n = 2k, so n² = 4k² = 2(2k²) is even. Therefore an n with n² odd cannot be even, so it must be odd.

What is a tautology? Show that (p ∧ q) → (p ∨ q) is a tautology.

Frequency: 2078 (Q4) · Marks: 5

A tautology is a compound proposition true for every assignment of truth values to its variables.

Test (p ∧ q) → (p ∨ q). An implication is false only when its antecedent is true and consequent false. If p ∧ q is true then both p and q are true, so p ∨ q is true; there is no row with true antecedent and false consequent.

pqp ∧ qp ∨ q(p ∧ q) → (p ∨ q)
TTTTT
TFFTT
FTFTT
FFFFT

The last column is all true, so the formula is a tautology.

Give four examples of sentences that are not propositions. Assume the premises: (i) All over-smart persons are stupid. (ii) Children of stupid persons are naughty. (iii) John is over-smart. (iv) Sam is a child of John. Using rules of inference, show that Sam is naughty.

Frequency: 2079 (Q1) · Marks: 10

A proposition is a declarative sentence with a definite truth value. Questions, commands, exclamations, open sentences, and paradoxes are not propositions: “Is it raining?”, “Close the door!”, “What a lovely day!”, “x + 2 = 5” (truth depends on x), and “This sentence is false”.

Let O(x) = “x is over-smart”, S(x) = “x is stupid”, N(x) = “x is naughty”, C(x, y) = “x is a child of y”. The premises are:

  1. ∀x(O(x) → S(x))
  2. ∀y∀x((S(y) ∧ C(x, y)) → N(x))
  3. O(John)
  4. C(Sam, John)

From (1) and (3), universal instantiation and modus ponens give S(John). From (2) with y = John and x = Sam we get (S(John) ∧ C(Sam, John)) → N(Sam). Both antecedents hold, so by modus ponens N(Sam): Sam is naughty.

Show by direct proof that the sum of two even numbers is even.

Frequency: 2079 (Q4) · Marks: 5

Let the two even numbers be a and b. By definition a = 2k and b = 2m for some integers k and m.

Then a + b = 2k + 2m = 2(k + m). Since k + m is an integer, a + b is twice an integer, and therefore even. The argument uses only the definition of evenness, so it holds for every pair of even integers.

Give the negations of the following statements:
  1. He has passed the exam.
  2. All dogs are loyal.
  3. Some medicine has side effects.
  4. If you study then you will pass the exam.
  5. Open the door.

Frequency: 2080.2 (Q4) · Marks: 5

Use ¬(∀x P(x)) ≡ ∃x ¬P(x), ¬(∃x P(x)) ≡ ∀x ¬P(x), and ¬(p → q) ≡ p ∧ ¬q.

  1. He has not passed the exam.
  2. Some dogs are not loyal.
  3. No medicine has side effects. (Equivalently: every medicine has no side effects.)
  4. You study and you will not pass the exam.
  5. “Open the door” is a command, not a proposition, so it has no truth value and no logical negation. Only declarative sentences can be negated.

Using a direct proof, show that the sum of the squares of even numbers is even.

Frequency: 2080.2 (Q7) · Marks: 5

Let the even numbers be 2k and 2m with k, m ∈ ℤ. Their squares are (2k)² = 4k² and (2m)² = 4m².

The sum is 4k² + 4m² = 2(2k² + 2m²). Because 2k² + 2m² is an integer, the sum is twice an integer and hence even.

This is stronger than needed: the sum of squares of any two integers with the same parity is even, since 4 divides each square here.

List any five rules of inference.

Frequency: 2080.2 (Q12) · Marks: 5

Five standard rules of inference:

  1. Modus ponens: from p and p → q infer q.
  2. Modus tollens: from ¬q and p → q infer ¬p.
  3. Hypothetical syllogism: from p → q and q → r infer p → r.
  4. Disjunctive syllogism: from p ∨ q and ¬p infer q.
  5. Resolution: from p ∨ q and ¬p ∨ r infer q ∨ r.

Each rule is a tautology when read as “(all premises) → conclusion”, so any application preserves truth.

Define propositions. Convert the following sentences into predicate logic:
  1. Some kind-hearted people still exist.
  2. Students who study hard and do their homework get good marks in exams.

Frequency: 2081 (Q4) · Marks: 5

A proposition is a declarative sentence that is either true or false, but not both. Predicate logic adds variables and quantifiers: ∀x for “every x” and ∃x for “some x”.

Let K(x) = “x is kind-hearted”, S(x) = “x studies hard”, H(x) = “x does homework”, and G(x) = “x gets good marks”.

  1. ∃x K(x) — at least one kind-hearted person exists.
  2. ∀x((S(x) ∧ H(x)) → G(x)) — every student who studies hard and does homework gets good marks.

The universal statement uses an implication, not a conjunction: it is vacuously true for people who fail the antecedent.

Using a direct proof, show that the sum of an odd number and an even number is odd.

Frequency: 2081 (Q10) · Marks: 5

Write the odd number as 2k + 1 and the even number as 2m, with k, m ∈ ℤ.

Their sum is (2k + 1) + 2m = 2(k + m) + 1. Since k + m is an integer, the sum has the form 2t + 1 with t = k + m, so it is odd.

This confirms the general parity rules: odd + even is odd, odd + odd and even + even are even.

Why do we need quantifiers? List any three rules of inference. Prove that √2 is irrational using proof by contradiction.

Frequency: 2082 (Q2) · Marks: 2+3+5

Quantifiers say how many objects a predicate covers. Without them “x is good” has no fixed truth value; ∀x and ∃x turn it into a proposition, and they let us formalise “every”, “some”, and “none” precisely.

Three rules of inference: modus ponens (p, p → q ⊢ q), universal instantiation (∀x P(x) ⊢ P(c)), and modus tollens (¬q, p → q ⊢ ¬p).

Proof that √2 is irrational. Suppose √2 = a/b in lowest terms with gcd(a, b) = 1. Squaring gives a² = 2b², so a² is even and hence a = 2k. Then 4k² = 2b² gives b² = 2k², so b is even too. Both a and b share the factor 2, contradicting gcd(a, b) = 1. The assumption fails, so √2 is irrational.
